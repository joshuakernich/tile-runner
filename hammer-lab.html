grid is squares per tile each way — at 4 a tile is sixteen pixels and the beam is four lanes wide. middle and outside are the two inner lanes and the two outer ones, which is what gives the line a core rather than a flat stripe. Lanes are measured off the beam's own axis, so a diagonal gets diagonal lanes. width is how wide the stream runs, in tiles, measured across the line — on a diagonal that width is what makes it a clean forty-five degree stream instead of a staircase of whole tiles.

The ripple

0.90

At travel 0 the middle simply stays brighter for as long as the tile stands. Above 0 it is how long the bright band takes to walk from the axis out to the outer lanes, so the beam comes apart sideways before it goes. band is how much of the width is lit at once — wide enough and the whole tile brightens and passes.

clear of him is how far in front the line has to start, in tiles — mid-stride he is between two cells, and this steps the near end over whichever one he is standing in so nothing is ever laid under his feet. step is the gap between one tile arriving and the next, capped by the shout: a long beam goes quicker per tile rather than still arriving after the shouting has stopped. The near end leaves first, so the line retreats the way it came — out step is that retreat's own pace, the gap between one tile leaving and the next, so the line can go out fast and dissolve slowly. Set it long and the shout will be over while the beam is still unmaking itself, which is allowed: the runner is already free by then.

The point

rim is how many squares deep the leading edge burns at the outside weight — one pixel by default, so the line ends in a thin dim border rather than a whole tile that quietly gave up. rounded tip takes the outer corners off that leading row, which is what keeps the wave from ending in a flat wall.

The tones

The two stone tones alternating square by square is what stops a long beam reading as one flat bar — but at grid 4 it is sixteen squares a tile, which may be more noise than the line wants.

Somewhere to start

core a hard bright spine, dim edges · blowout a fast ripple that throws the line apart sideways · slow ripple the same, walked out over the whole hold · coarse the old two-by-two · flat no lanes at all, for comparison.

Try it

Take it away

The runner stands in the middle of a field of stone, crawling so he stays put, and swings on a loop. Every stone the line lands on takes one hit as it arrives — the same as a tap — so a three-hit stone will stand there cracked with the beam lying across it. When the field is cleared the board rebuilds itself.

The cell here is the size it is on a mid-sized level, so the squares are the size you will actually see them. Nothing is redrawn: the lab pushes the HAMMER block into the running game through __TR.hammer() and fires through __TR.swing(), so this is the shipped renderer and not a copy of it that could drift.
