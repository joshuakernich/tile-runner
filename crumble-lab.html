Three black plates, each inset further into the tile than the last, so the hole has a floor you can see down to instead of being one flat square. Each darkens what is already under it — the step edges are what tell you there is a bottom. outer is the edge of the plates; the sub-tiles above them use the whole cell. Either way nothing this tile draws reaches past its own square, whatever you set — a Singularity can never touch the tile next door. grain is one sheet of the game's own paper over the whole hole; put it under the plates instead and each step carries its own texture, which reads as three sheets rather than one thing with a bottom. tremble is how hard the floor shakes; everything else is scaled off it by size, so the smaller a shape is the harder it moves and the outer plate never moves at all. A sub-tile, smaller than any plate, gets more of it than the floor does. It runs on its own clock, deliberately not the draw's.

Sub-tiles the size of one square of a grid cut across the whole cell — the tile's own edge, not the well's — appearing on a square of its rim and pulled smoothly in to the middle. The grid sets where a sub-tile comes from and how big it is; it is not a track it steps along, so the pull is a slide rather than a march. Which rim square one comes in on is picked afresh every trip, so the pattern never comes round. ease above 1 dawdles at the rim then lets go; below 1 leaves at once and creeps the last of the way in. opacity is only where a sub-tile appears — it always lands at the floor's own, whatever that is, so it arrives rather than winking out.

The glow

White at the middle going to yellow at the edge, behind everything else — the one warm thing on a tile that is otherwise pure absence. It is drawn before the clip, so what falls inside the well is buried under the three plates and what falls outside is all of it that escapes. reach is a fraction of a whole CELL, so past 0.50 the glow spills over the tile's edge and onto its neighbours; below that it stays in, and only the plates dim it.

Six Singularities on a three-column board, at real size, with the runner crawling up the middle so the pair beside him are always the same size they are in a level. They sit on the bare floor: a Singularity is not a tile, it is the absence of one, which is why the tile you cross it on is the price of crossing.
