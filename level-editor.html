Start is the one cell that begins as laid track — every level has exactly one.
Tile drops another of the runner's tiles on the board — click one again to take it away, and drag any of them freely. A level does not carry a starting route: tiles lined up out of the start chain themselves into track when it loads (drawn solid, with the front ringed), and any tile left elsewhere stays loose (drawn hollow) until the player drags it into place. Scatter them around the level if you want them found rather than given. Four tiles including the start is the norm.
Goal: click the flag's own cell to remove it, or rub it out with the eraser — a level with no flag never ends, so the run only stops when the runner falls. A talisman maze uses the charm as its exit and carries no flag at all.
Slider block: one click drops the block on a 3-cell rail, with a nook each side. Drag the block to slide it along its rail (that's its starting cell); drag it past an end or sideways off the rail and the whole structure moves. Then drag either end handle (the ringed dots) to lengthen, shorten or swing the rail round — pull an end sideways and the rail rotates. The rail is where the block slides to; it needs a dead-end nook on the far side.
Saw: one click drops a saw on a 3-cell rail (uses the speed + phase boxes); clicking an existing saw re-applies those boxes to it. Drag either end handle to lengthen, shorten or swing the rail round — it pivots on the opposite end. Drag the blade along the rail to set its starting phase (0 = parked at the low end, 0.5 = at the high end) — stagger a row of saws this way so they don't sweep as one line. Drag the blade past an end or off the line, or drag any other rail cell, to move the whole saw.
Monster: pick the kind first. Horizontal and vertical rail monsters slide along a rail exactly as the saws they replaced — drop one and drag its ends. A square loop circles a ring; a hunter is a malevolent tile entity that comes for the runner — it walks the shortest open route to him, around walls rather than into them, and re-thinks it at every cell; a trap sits still, yawns open wide enough to run through, then snaps shut on whatever is in it. Clicking the square a monster is standing on re-applies the kind box; clicking anywhere else on its route drops a new monster there, so routes may cross and overlap freely. Only patrol is drawn by hand: click each cell of its route in turn — the first click starts the path, every click after adds a waypoint. Enter finishes it as a there-and-back patrol; L closes it into a loop; Esc throws the draft away. It moves at the runner's own pace and pauses briefly at each waypoint, and touching it is fatal.
Stone: drag to paint breakable stone blocks (the hp box sets how many taps to smash). Right-drag or drag over a stone to remove.
Rail: fixed free track cast into a wall. Click an empty cell to place it in the selected shape; click it again to cycle through the six shapes; right-click to remove. The runner rides it for free but can only enter/leave through the two drawn ends — the reachability check knows this. Rails override the player's route, so a level built on them needs the forced-chain check, not plain reachability.
Talisman: a one-off collectible. Click to place the one chosen in the dropdown, click again to cycle, right-click to remove. It does not block track — put it somewhere the runner can flow through, never at the end of a spur, or it can't be collected.
Coin: a golden tile worth one tile of shop money. Click to drop one, click again to remove.
Key: pick a colour swatch first — a key opens the door of the same colour.
Door: pick a colour swatch first — it opens for the key of the same colour.

Drag the map's edges to resize it. The right and bottom edges just add or remove columns and rows; the left and top edges push everything across as they grow, so the map expands in that direction instead of the far one. Shrinking drops whatever falls outside.

What this is

Every line of writing the game says that is not part of a level — the cards that introduce a mechanic, and the headings and lines on a fall, a pause and a clear. It lives in copy.js, which the game loads exactly the way it loads levels.js. The verses on the chapter cards are not here: those belong to their level, and you edit them on the Levels tab.

Lists are one entry per line. Blank lines are dropped.

Mechanic cards

Shown once, the first time a chapter uses the thing. The key is what a level's intro list names — it is not copy, so it is not editable here; renaming one would orphan every level that asks for it.
