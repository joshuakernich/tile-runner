At peak 0 the heap is a straight-sided triangle — every row two bodies narrower than the one below, which is what it always was. At 1 the silhouette follows a Lorentzian: bodies are lost slowly at the bottom and then all at once near the top, so it is a spire standing out of a long low skirt rather than a cone. sharpness is the curve's half-width at half its height, as a share of the base's — small is a needle with wide tails, large is a broad dome. The slider crossfades between the two shapes, so anything in between is reachable.

Rows, each two bodies narrower than the one below it, stacked and offset like brickwork — the triangle is the structure, not luck. rows is the heap at your FIRST fall; it gains one every three attempts up to rows max, and the base widens to match so it still comes to a point. across and up are the spacing, in fractions of a cell: this is the gapless knob — take them below the body's own width and height and the bodies overlap into a solid mass. skirt is how many full-width rows carry on below the base and off the bottom of the frame, so the mountain has no visible underside.

Two things decide where in the frame it sits, and the lower of them wins. summit at puts the peak that far down the view; base past pushes the bottom row that many cells beyond the foot of the screen. With the skirt at 0 the base IS the bottom of the mound, so it is base past that keeps it running off the edge rather than floating above it — turn it up if you ever see sky underneath.

A body is one flat hue off the wheel and nothing else — no drip. The shop's finishes glow, streak, facet and split down the middle, and thirty of those in one heap read as a pile of special effects: the eye goes to the two that are lit instead of to the mound. hue from and hue span cut the wheel down if you want a heap in one family, and at saturation 0 the hue stops mattering altogether and the heap is greys — in which case light span is the whole of the variety, spreading the bodies either side of lightness from near-black to white. hats at 0 takes the hats off too and leaves plain squares.

Every body is drawn as a SQUARE WITH EYES — no legs, no kicks. A heap made of running figures is mostly leg, and a leg is a thin line, so however tightly the rows were packed the mound came out full of holes with a thicket of shins over them. at the top is a body at the summit and deeper is how much bigger one at the front of the base is; nothing here is in perspective, but that size ramp is the cheapest way to say the peak is further away. wander is how far one may stray from its place — enough to stop the stack looking stamped, and the first thing to turn down if you are chasing gaps.

How many deaths in

The heap grows a row every three attempts, so it is a different mountain on your first fall and your tenth. These stage the fall at that count without making you die there — the scatter is a hash, not a random number, so the heap you get is the heap the game would have built.
